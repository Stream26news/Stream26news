CNN, MS NOW and Politico Sue Trump Administration

Verified Gerald Anthony · 5 min read
CNN, MS NOW and Politico have filed suit over their White House ban
Share f 𝕏 wa tg

WASHINGTON — CNN, MS NOW and Politico filed a federal lawsuit against the Trump administration on Monday, September 21, challenging the White House decision to bar their journalists from its grounds after President Donald Trump accused the outlets of producing what he described as “fake news” and said the restrictions were necessary to protect national security.

The legal action came three days after Trump announced that the three news organisations would no longer be permitted to operate from the White House under the administration's press-access arrangements. The dispute has since widened beyond the three organisations involved, becoming a broader confrontation over how presidential press access is administered and the constitutional protections surrounding independent journalism.

The lawsuit was filed in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia. CNN, MS NOW and Politico argue that the administration's decision improperly interferes with their ability to report on the president and his administration and violates protections guaranteed by the First Amendment.

The White House has defended the decision. Trump has repeatedly argued that the administration is not attacking freedom of the press but is instead responding to organisations he considers unfair or hostile in their coverage. On Monday, he repeated that position on Truth Social, describing what he called “fake news” as a national-security concern.

The confrontation has already affected the traditional White House media pool. Several major television networks suspended participation in the presidential pool arrangement after the ban, creating an unusual situation in which the White House's normal system for distributing video coverage of presidential events was disrupted.

The dispute became particularly visible on Monday when Trump held an event connected with the newly constructed White House helipad. Major networks did not provide their usual live pool coverage, while the president's activities continued to be communicated through other official and social-media channels.

The administration's decision follows a series of disputes during Trump's presidency over which news organisations should receive access to White House officials and presidential events. The latest action, however, directly affects three major outlets simultaneously and has prompted a legal challenge centred on press access and constitutional rights.

The case is expected to test the limits of presidential control over access to the White House and whether an administration can restrict individual media organisations because of disagreement with their reporting. The government will have an opportunity to respond to the lawsuit as the court considers the organisations' request for relief.

The controversy also arrives at a particularly prominent moment in the presidential calendar. Trump is in New York for the United Nations General Assembly, where he is scheduled to meet foreign leaders and participate in a series of diplomatic engagements. The president is also preparing for a state visit by Chinese President Xi Jinping later in the week.

For the White House, the dispute represents another confrontation with major news organisations over media access. For the three plaintiffs, the lawsuit is an effort to restore access and establish that government officials cannot determine which news organisations may cover the president based on disagreement with their reporting.
