Meta Heads to Trial Over Claims It Designed Instagram and Facebook to Hook Young Users

Verified Stream26 Global Desk · 4 min read
G GLOBAL
A courtroom test of how much tech platforms knew about their effect on kids

Meta is in a California courtroom this week, defending itself against claims that it deliberately engineered Instagram and Facebook to maximize engagement among minors, aware of the potential harm to their mental health.

The case centers on internal product decisions dating back several years — from how notifications are timed and worded, to how recommendation algorithms surface content, to design choices around features like infinite scroll and disappearing messages. Plaintiffs argue these were not neutral engineering choices but deliberate design decisions made with knowledge of their effect on young, still-developing attention spans.

Meta has consistently and publicly defended its safety measures, pointing to features like parental supervision tools, teen account restrictions, and default privacy settings for younger users introduced over the past several years. The company has also argued more broadly that a wave of similar lawsuits nationwide risks holding tech platforms responsible for complex mental health outcomes with many contributing causes beyond any one product.

This trial is one of the more closely watched of the wave of youth-safety lawsuits filed against major platforms in recent years, and legal analysts say its outcome could shape how courts handle similar cases still working through the system.

The stakes reach beyond the courtroom. Regulators in a number of African countries are currently drafting their own child-online-safety frameworks, and several have said they are watching how U.S. courts handle the question of platform design responsibility as they finalize their own rules.

Stream26 News has not independently reviewed Meta's full legal filings or the plaintiffs' expert evidence, and will report on the verdict once it is announced.
