Ebola Outbreak in Eastern Congo Raises Fears Over School Reopening

Developing Story Stream26 Africa Desk · 4 min read
AFRICA
No approved vaccine, and a September 1 school reopening date in question

Parents and teachers in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo are calling for schools to delay reopening as health authorities battle a rapidly expanding Ebola outbreak. Schools in the affected area are scheduled to reopen September 1, but communities fear returning thousands of children to classrooms could increase transmission risk.

The concern is especially serious because the outbreak involves a rare Bundibugyo virus strain for which there is currently no approved vaccine or treatment. Health workers already face a difficult operating environment: insecurity, population displacement, and movement between communities have complicated efforts to identify cases and trace contacts.

The outbreak's scale has increased pressure on an international response that humanitarian organisations say remains underfunded. Parents are caught between two real concerns — children need education, but families also fear bringing an infectious disease home. That fear has produced calls for authorities to postpone reopening until adequate protective measures are in place.

The challenge extends well beyond closing classrooms: it requires surveillance, testing, isolation, contact tracing, treatment, and community cooperation. Public trust matters enormously here, since fear can cause people to avoid health facilities or conceal contact with infected individuals.

Stream26 News understands the situation remains fluid, with health authorities continuing to assess the spread. The latest figures indicate a severe outbreak, with further updates expected as surveillance continues. For now, the question confronting eastern Congo is whether schools can reopen without creating another pathway for transmission.

Still developing: Case and transmission figures for this outbreak are still being compiled and may change as surveillance continues. No approved vaccine or treatment currently exists for this viral strain.
