Nigeria's Kidnapping Economy Reaches Alarming Scale Across Multiple Regions

Verified Stream26 Nigeria Desk · 7 min read
AFRICA
7,825 abducted. ₦7.78 billion paid. A criminal economy, not just a crime wave.

Nigeria's kidnapping crisis has become a multi-billion-naira criminal economy, with 7,825 people abducted and at least 1,142 people killed in a single 12-month period, according to new research reviewed by Stream26 News. The figures cover July 2025 to June 2026 and reveal both the scale of the country's abduction crisis and the scale of money now flowing to armed groups through ransom.

At least ₦7.78 billion in confirmed ransom payments was recorded during the period — roughly $5.79 million at the exchange rate used in the research. The real total paid by families is likely higher, since many ransom payments are never publicly disclosed.

A total of 1,713 kidnapping and kidnapping-related incidents were recorded, a sharp deterioration from the prior reporting cycle. Victim numbers rose 66%, from 4,722 to 7,825. Strikingly, the amount kidnappers demanded actually fell, from ₦48 billion to about ₦22.9 billion — but the collection rate rose from roughly 5.35% to 34%, meaning kidnappers asked for less overall but successfully collected far more of it.

A small number of mass-abduction cases account for most of the money. The Jama'atu Ahlis Sunna Lidda'awati Wal-Jihad faction of Boko Haram accounted for roughly 90% of recorded ransom payments. Two cases stand out: the abduction of 416 people in Ngoshe, Borno State, tied to an estimated ₦5 billion ransom, and the abduction of 315 pupils and staff from St Mary's School in Papiri, Niger State, tied to an estimated ₦2 billion.

The crisis spans multiple regions rather than one hotspot. Researchers identified the north-west and north-central as major centres of kidnapping activity, while the north-east presents a distinct picture shaped by the long-running insurgency. Security analyst Kehinde Giwa has warned that the ransom market itself fuels the cycle, cautioning that paying kidnappers risks “birthing another kidnapping incident.”

Nigeria has recorded real successes too — including a recent operation that rescued 308 abducted citizens from Kwara and Niger states, which President Tinubu welcomed publicly. But rescue operations, however successful, respond after an abduction has already happened. The harder, unresolved question is whether intelligence and early-warning systems can be built to prevent kidnappings before they occur, rather than only recovering victims afterward.

“you are simply birthing another kidnapping incident”Kehinde Giwa, security analyst
Still developing: These figures are research-based estimates compiled from documented incidents, interviews, and public information — not a complete national census. Because many incidents and ransom payments go unreported, the true scale may be higher than recorded here.
