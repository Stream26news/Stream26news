Hakainde Hichilema Wins Second Term as President of Zambia

Verified Stream26 Africa Desk · 3 min read
A AFRICA
A second term, and a test of whether economic recovery reaches ordinary Zambians

Zambia's Electoral Commission has declared Hakainde Hichilema the winner of a second presidential term, with all 226 constituencies counted. Hichilema secured 2,965,326 votes, or 60.49%, against 1,856,217 votes, or 37.87%, for his closest challenger, opposition leader Brian Mundubile, comfortably clearing the constitutional 50%-plus-one threshold needed to avoid a runoff.

Commission chairperson Mwangala Zaloumis formally declared the result in the early hours of Tuesday morning. It was Hichilema's seventh run for the presidency going back over two decades, while it was Mundubile's first bid for the country's top office after entering the race in May against a crowded field of 14 candidates.

The count itself was not without incident. Vote tallying was suspended at one point after violence broke out at polling stations, and opposition officials say they were briefly detained during a law enforcement raid in which gunshots were fired. International and local observers are still compiling their formal assessments of the vote's conduct, and Stream26 News has not independently reviewed those reports.

Turnout is estimated at roughly 57 to 70% depending on which registration baseline is used, a detail election analysts say still needs to be reconciled against final published figures. What is clear is that Mundubile's showing, at nearly 38%, was stronger than most pre-election commentary had anticipated, reflecting public frustration over the cost of living even as Zambia's broader economy has stabilized.

Hichilema, 64, first took office in 2021 in the middle of a pandemic-era debt crisis that made Zambia the first African country to default on its sovereign debt during that period. His first term centered on restructuring that debt and stabilizing the economy of Africa's second-largest copper producer — a turnaround international investors have broadly welcomed, even as many ordinary Zambians say they have yet to feel it in their daily cost of living.

That gap between macroeconomic recovery and household-level relief is now widely seen as the central test of Hichilema's second term. Stream26 News will continue following the story, including any formal observer findings on the conduct of the vote.

Still developing: Independent international and local observer assessments of the vote's conduct had not yet been published in full at time of writing. Turnout figures also vary depending on which voter-registration baseline is used, and should be treated as approximate until reconciled.
