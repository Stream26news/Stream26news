UK Air Traffic Failure Disrupts Flights Again

Verified Staff of Stream26 News · 5 min read
A new fault at Prestwick air traffic control disrupted flights across Britain
Share f 𝕏 wa tg

LONDON — A new connectivity failure at the UK's Prestwick air traffic control centre disrupted flights across parts of Britain on Monday, September 21, affecting airports including Manchester, Glasgow and Belfast and prompting airlines to raise fresh concerns about the resilience of the country's air traffic-control infrastructure.

The problem occurred at the National Air Traffic Services facility at Prestwick in Scotland and caused delays and cancellations as controllers experienced difficulties with the system used to manage air traffic. London Gatwick and London City airports also reported disruption as the problem affected flight operations beyond Scotland.

Transport Minister Heidi Alexander said the issue had been resolved and that the systems were back online. However, the disruption continued to affect passengers as airlines worked through the resulting backlog.

Ryanair said approximately 25,000 passengers were affected and renewed its criticism of the resilience of the air traffic-control system. EasyJet also reported cancellations and criticised the reliability of the infrastructure, while British Airways warned passengers that delays could continue as operations returned to normal.

The incident was particularly significant because it came less than two weeks after another air traffic-control failure caused widespread disruption across Britain. That earlier incident was linked to a software defect and resulted in around 2,000 flights being cancelled, creating major disruption for airlines and passengers.

By the morning of September 21, at least 24 flights had been cancelled, while many more experienced delays. Airlines were required to reorganise schedules and aircraft movements while air traffic controllers worked to restore normal operations.

The Civil Aviation Authority said passengers affected by disruption should receive the care required under passenger-protection rules, although compensation depends on the specific circumstances surrounding a delay or cancellation.

The problems have placed additional attention on NATS, the organisation responsible for managing air traffic services across the United Kingdom. Airlines have argued that repeated technical problems can impose significant costs on carriers and passengers, particularly when disruption occurs during busy travel periods.

The September incident also revived wider concerns about the reliability of critical national infrastructure. Air traffic control relies on highly integrated communication and data systems, meaning that a technical fault can have consequences far beyond the facility where the problem originates.

Although Monday's system failure was resolved, the timing of the incident has ensured that attention remains on the robustness of the UK's air traffic-control arrangements and the measures being taken to prevent similar failures.
